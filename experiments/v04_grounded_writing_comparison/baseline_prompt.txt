Second stage: downscaling\n3.2.1. Downscale-UNet\nAn advanced convolutional neural network based on the UNet ar -\nchitecture is developed in this stage called as Downscale-UNet. The\nmodel network employs a symmetric encoder-decoder structure with\nskip connections, combining the advantages of hierarchical feature\nextraction and precise spatial reconstruction ( Ronneberger et al., 2015 ),\nthereby particularly suitable for our aerosol downscaling.\nIn this study, we introduce the Convolutional Block Attention Mod -\nule (CBAM) for UNet. The module is a simple but effective attention\nmodule for feed-forward convolutional neural networks ( Woo et al.,\n2018 ). The CBAM can sequentially infer the attention map along the\nchannel and spatial dimensions and then multiply the attention map\nwith the input feature map for adaptive feature optimization to improve\ntraining speed and prediction accuracy ( Fig. 1 a-b). Since CBAM is a\nlightweight and general-purpose module, the overhead of the module\ncan be ignored and seamlessly integrated into any CNN-like architecture\nand can be trained end-to-end with the basic UNet model (Test S1).\nAdditionally, in order to alleviate the number of parameters in the UNet\nmodel and to ensure the timeliness of aerosol downscaling, we replace\nthe conventional convolution operation with a Depthwise Separable\nConvolution (DSC) module in the basic UNet model ( Chollet, 2017 ). DSC\nconsists of depth-wise convolution and point-wise convolution ( Fig. 1 c).\nThe computation of depth-wise convolution is simple; it uses a convo -\nlution kernel for each channel of the input feature map, and then the\noutputs of all the convolution kernels are then spliced together to get its\nfinal output; While the traditional convolution has to perform a k-\nconvolution for all k channels and finally output the result for each\nchannel ( Fig. 1 d) which significantly increases the amount of compu -\ntation compared to the DSC operation. By construct, point-wise convo -\nlution is actually a 1 × 1 convolution, which plays two roles in DSC. Discussion and conclusions\nUtilizing datasets form CERES and MODIS satellites, we analyzed the\nspatial distribution and temporal trends of AOD and ADRF in the zonal\nregion between 20\n◦\nN and 40\n◦\nN. The results show that the spatial dis -\ntributions of ADRF and AOD are closely related. Aerosols cool the sur -\nface and heat the atmosphere, imposing a cooling effect at the top of the\natmosphere. The strongest ADRFs are located in the Eastern China,\nNorthern India and North Africa where dust or anthropogenic aerosols\nare originated. The areas with the weakest ADRFs are located in areas\nwhere AOD is the lowest such as the oceans and the plateaus.\nWe also found distinct seasonal variations of ADRF.
