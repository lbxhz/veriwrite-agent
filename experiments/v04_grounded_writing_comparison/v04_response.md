## 气溶胶遥感反演方法、应用与局限

Aerosols profoundly influence climate, environmental quality, and human health, making accurate aerosol mapping essential for understanding their interactions with meteorological and epidemiological factors, particularly in regions with sparse ground stations. However, existing aerosol products derived from satellite retrievals and model reanalyses face significant limitations in spatial coverage and resolution, hindering their utility for local-scale applications and process studies. [@sun2026_mosres2025108411, pp. 1, 10]

To address these gaps, recent studies have developed advanced downscaling and fusion algorithms. For instance, a two-stage hybrid algorithm combines ensemble learning with an enhanced UNet model (termed Downscale-UNet) to generate high-resolution, seamless AOD maps over China. The first stage uses ensemble learning to fill gaps in MODIS AOD and correct MERRA-2 AOD at the pixel level, creating reliable training labels and spatially consistent input features. The second stage employs an enhanced UNet incorporating Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) and Depthwise Separable Convolution (DSC) to extract spatial features and produce the final downscaled product. The model demonstrated robust performance on unseen data, achieving an R² of 0.93, MAE of 0.04, and RMSE of 0.06, with over 95% of predictions within the expected error range and negligible bias. Ablation experiments confirmed the contributions of CBAM and DSC modules to the model's performance. [@sun2026_mosres2025108411, pp. 6, 10]

Complementing daytime retrievals, geostationary satellite data have been exploited to estimate nighttime AOD using machine learning. By leveraging infrared (IR) channels that observe both day and night, researchers have developed models to estimate nighttime AOD over East Asia. The Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LGBM) outperformed Random Forest (RF) in all-day repeated double cross-validation, with a slope closer to 1 and higher fractions within expected error. This difference is attributed to LGBM's bias-reducing nature versus RF's variance-reducing approach, leading to RF's underestimation in high AOD ranges. The proposed satellite-based nighttime AOD product offers better spatial resolution compared to existing reanalysis or forecast-based products, such as CAMS and MERRA-2, which are typically coarser. The estimated AOD spatial distribution showed good agreement with other products, and the time series captured diurnal variability. [@song2025_mosenv2025121365, pp. 1, 6, 12]

Satellite-derived AOD has also been applied to estimate aerosol direct radiative forcing (ADRF) and understand its climate implications. Using CERES and MODIS data over the 20°N–40°N zonal region, studies show that the spatial distributions of ADRF and AOD are closely related. Regional averaged ADRFs are negative (cooling) at the surface and top of atmosphere (TOA), and positive (warming) in the atmosphere. Higher ADRFs appear from February to May, due to larger AOD in spring and summer. The strongest ADRFs are located in Eastern China, Northern India, and North Africa, where dust or anthropogenic aerosols originate, while the weakest occur over oceans and plateaus with low AOD. These findings highlight the role of aerosols in cooling the surface and heating the atmosphere, with a net cooling at TOA. [@chen2022_mosres2021105938, pp. 1, 9, 10]

Collectively, these studies illustrate the evolution of aerosol remote sensing from addressing data gaps and resolution limitations to enabling novel applications such as nighttime monitoring and radiative forcing assessment. The integration of machine learning with multi-source satellite and reanalysis data has proven effective in generating seamless, high-resolution AOD products. However, the limited geographical scope of these studies (e.g., China, East Asia) and the reliance on specific satellite sensors warrant further validation for global applicability. Additionally, while the improved products enhance our ability to assess aerosol impacts, their application to health and climate studies remains an area of active research, constrained by the inherent uncertainties in aerosol retrievals and the complexity of radiative transfer.
